# Abstract (≤ 150 words)

We classify the construction-age class of 30 m Landsat pixels from 42-year time series, training on Madrid and transferring to Amsterdam with few labels. Rebuilding the input — removing broken scenes and saturated values, and replacing the first-photo rule with a warm-season median composite — cut year-to-year noise by 40–46 %. We engineered 321 label-free features (spectral indices, robust trends, one- and two-break construction change-points, relative and spatial-context features) and standardised each city with its own statistics. A LightGBM–XGBoost–Random Forest ensemble reaches macro F1 0.652 ± 0.026 on Madrid under strict spatial-block cross-validation. For Amsterdam, a CatBoost base model is adapted by recalibration, weighted fine-tuning and self-training on unlabelled pixels, then ensembled, prior-corrected when labels suffice, and spatially smoothed. Macro F1 rises from 0.654 (n = 5) to 0.687 (n = 25) and 0.757 (n = 200), beating the organisers' starter baseline by 0.09–0.15.
